Best practices for recording treadmill walking and running.

**Minimizing body segment occlusion is critical** — every segment must be visible to at least two cameras at all times. Adjust camera positions based on your treadmill configuration.

:::tip
Before finalizing your setup, have the participant perform the activity while you monitor each camera's field of view. Adjust tripod positions to minimize occlusions from guardrails, the treadmill frame, or other equipment — every body segment must remain visible on at least two screens throughout the movement.
:::

## 1. Recording Setup

Choose the setup that fits your treadmill configuration, in order of preference:

### a. Cameras facing the front of the participant (recommended)

Use this setup when the treadmill is not against a wall, the equipment does not occlude the participant, or the treadmill supports a reverse mode. Have the participant **face the cameras**.

Position the cameras at **~30–40° from the participant's forward-facing direction**, then adjust as needed to reduce occlusions caused by guardrails.

### b. Cameras facing the back of the participant

Use this setup when the treadmill is against a wall, has a large front-facing display, or does not support a reverse mode. Have the participant **face away from the cameras**.

Position the cameras at **~30–40° from the participant's forward-facing direction**, then adjust as needed to reduce occlusions caused by guardrails.

## 2. Calibration

The checkerboard does not need to be aligned with the treadmill — simply ensure it is **fully visible to all cameras** and **perpendicular to the ground**.

## 3. Static pose

Have the participant face the same direction as they will during the recording. **If using the back-facing setup, perform the static pose with the participant facing away from the cameras as well**.
