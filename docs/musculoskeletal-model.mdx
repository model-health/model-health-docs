:::note
This page is a technical reference covering joint coordinate systems, degrees of freedom, and anatomical definitions.
:::

Our platform is built on [OpenSim](https://opensim.stanford.edu/), a widely used open-source musculoskeletal simulation framework with over 15 years of use across research and clinical settings [[Delp et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18018689/), [Seth et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30048444/)]. We use a scientifically-validated model with 36 degrees of freedom [[Rajagopal 2016](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27392337/), [Lai et al. 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28900782/), [Uhlrich et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35798755/)] that incorporates physiologically accurate joint definitions — joints are constrained to move in ways that reflect real human anatomy (e.g., the hip rotates in three directions but cannot translate, like a ball-and-socket joint).

![Musculoskeletal model overview](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lai-uhlrich-2022-shoulder.png)

## Joint Definitions

### Ground–Pelvis

6 degrees of freedom — 3 translations and 3 rotations. Defines how the pelvis moves relative to the world frame (defined by the checkerboard).

**Rotations**

- **Pelvis tilt** — forward and backward tipping of the pelvis. Positive when the pelvis rotates backward.

  ![Pelvis tilt](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-tilt.png)

- **Pelvis list** — side-to-side tilting of the pelvis. Positive when the left part of the pelvis tilts upward.

  ![Pelvis list](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-list.png)

- **Pelvis rotation** — twisting of the pelvis around the vertical axis. Positive when the pelvis rotates to the left.

  ![Pelvis rotation](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-rotation.png)

:::note Biomechanics detail
Pelvis rotations follow ZXY (body-fixed) Euler angle convention: tilt → list → rotation.
:::

**Translations**

- **Pelvis tx** — forward/backward translation. Positive = forward.

  ![Pelvis tx](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-tx.png)

- **Pelvis ty** — up/down translation. Positive = upward.

  ![Pelvis ty](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-ty.png)

- **Pelvis tz** — side-to-side translation. Positive = rightward.

  ![Pelvis tz](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/pelvis-tz.png)

### Hip

3 degrees of freedom — 3 rotations. Defines how the femur moves relative to the pelvis.

- **Hip flexion-extension** — positive = flexion, negative = extension.

  ![Left hip flexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-flexion-l.png) ![Left hip extension](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-extension-l.png)

- **Hip adduction-abduction** — positive = adduction, negative = abduction.

  ![Left hip adduction](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-adduction-l.png) ![Left hip abduction](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-abduction-l.png)

- **Hip internal-external rotation** — positive = internal rotation, negative = external rotation.

  ![Left hip internal rotation](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-rotation-l.png) ![Left hip external rotation](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/hip-external-rotation-l.png)

:::note Biomechanics detail
Hip rotations follow ZXY (body-fixed) Euler angle convention: flexion → adduction → rotation.
:::

---

### Knee

1 degree of freedom. Defines how the tibia moves relative to the femur.

- **Knee flexion-extension** — positive = flexion, 0 = full extension.

  ![Left knee flexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/knee-flexion-l.png)

:::note Biomechanics detail
The knee isn't a simple hinge. While described by a single flexion-extension coordinate, it also involves small coupled rotations and translations. This means the lower leg shifts slightly inward during deep flexion.

  ![Knee neutral](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/knee-back.png) ![Deep knee flexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/knee-flexion-back-r.png)
:::

---

### Ankle

1 degree of freedom. Defines how the talus moves relative to the tibia.

- **Ankle dorsi-plantarflexion** — positive = dorsiflexion, negative = plantarflexion.

  ![Left ankle dorsiflexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/ankle-dorsi-l.png) ![Left ankle plantarflexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/ankle-plantar-l.png)

---

### Subtalar

1 degree of freedom. Defines how the calcaneus moves relative to the talus.

- **Foot supination-pronation** — positive = supination, negative = pronation.

  ![Left foot supination](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/subt-supination-l.png) ![Left foot pronation](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/subt-pronation-l.png)

---

### Lumbar

3 degrees of freedom — 3 rotations. Defines how the torso moves relative to the pelvis (at the L5 level).

- **Lumbar flexion-extension** — positive = extension, negative = flexion.

  ![Lumbar extension](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-extension.png) ![Lumbar flexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-flexion.png)

- **Lumbar bending** — positive = left shoulder moves upward, negative = right shoulder moves upward.

  ![Lumbar bending positive](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-bending-pos.png) ![Lumbar bending negative](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-bending-neg.png)

- **Lumbar rotation** — positive = torso rotates leftward, negative = torso rotates rightward.

  ![Lumbar rotation positive](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-rotation-pos.png) ![Lumbar rotation negative](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/lumbar-rotation-neg.png)

:::note Biomechanics detail
Lumbar rotations follow ZXY (body-fixed) Euler angle convention: flexion → bending → rotation.
:::

---

### Shoulder

6 degrees of freedom — 3 translations and 3 rotations. Models the full shoulder complex including the scapulothoracic segment.

**Translations** (scapulothoracic segment relative to torso)

- **Shoulder tx** — forward/backward translation. Positive = forward.

  ![Shoulder tx](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-tx.png)

- **Shoulder ty** — up/down translation. Positive = upward.

  ![Shoulder ty](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-ty.png)

- **Shoulder tz** — side-to-side translation. Positive = medially.

  ![Shoulder tz](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-tz.png)

**Rotations** (glenohumeral joint — humerus relative to scapulothoracic body)

- **Shoulder plane elevation** — angle of the plane in which the arm lifts. Positive = in front of torso, negative = behind torso.

  ![Plane elevation 50°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-plane-elev.50.png) ![Plane elevation 0°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-plane-elev.0.png) ![Plane elevation -50°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-plane-elev.-50.png)

- **Shoulder elevation** — how much the arm lifts away from the body within the current plane. Positive = arm raising.

  ![Elevation 0°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-elev.0.png) ![Elevation 90°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-elev.90.png) ![Elevation 150°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-elev.150.png)

- **Shoulder axial rotation** — twisting of the upper arm around its own axis. Positive = internal rotation, negative = external rotation.

  ![Axial rotation 50°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-axial-rot.50.png) ![Axial rotation 0°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-axial-rot.0.png) ![Axial rotation -50°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/shoulder-axial-rot.-50.png)

:::note Biomechanics detail
Shoulder rotations follow YXY (body-fixed) Euler angle convention: plane elevation → elevation → axial rotation. This is recommended by the [International Society of Biomechanics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15844264/).
:::

---

### Elbow

2 degrees of freedom — 2 rotations. Defines ulna movement relative to humerus and radius movement relative to ulna.

- **Elbow flexion-extension** — positive = flexion, 0 = full extension.

  ![Left elbow flexion](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/elbow-flexion-l.png)

- **Forearm pronation-supination** — positive = pronation (palm down), 0 = full supination (palm up).

  ![Forearm pronation 0°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/elbow-pronation-0-l.png) ![Forearm pronation 50°](/img/docs/musculoskeletal-model/elbow-pronation-50-l.png)
