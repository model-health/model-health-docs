:::tip
This page covers high-level best practices for collecting high-quality biomechanical data with Model Health.
More specific advice on camera placement and calibration can be found in the [Recording Guides](/docs/recording-small-capture) section.
:::

## General

- Ensure you have a stable internet connection. Video upload may fail with a poor connection.
- Do not move the cameras after calibration. If a camera is accidentally moved, create a new setup.
- The checkerboard is only used for calibration. You can remove it once the calibration step is complete.

## Camera Setup

- Keep the Model Health app updated on each device to get the latest recording features.
- Use tripods to hold each device in a fixed position during the session.
- The rear-facing camera is used for recording — position devices accordingly.
- Position the cameras to **avoid body segment occlusions** during the task.
  - At least two cameras need to see every body segment at all times. Two cameras is sufficient for many tasks, but tasks where segments get occluded may benefit from an additional front-facing camera (e.g., single-leg activities).
  - For a two-camera setup, cameras at 30–40° off the forward-facing line are typically best. Pure sagittal views do not work well due to limb occlusion.
- **Avoid people in the background.** Our algorithms handle this, but it makes them slower and more error-prone.
- Do a short test with the participant performing the task and **check that the participant remains visible in each camera for the entire activity before calibrating**.
- Position the smartphone in **either landscape or portrait** orientation. If in landscape, make sure **portrait orientation is not locked** in your device settings.
- **Avoid having the participant exit and re-enter the capture volume.**

## Camera Calibration

**Environment:**
- Record in a well-lit environment.
- If recording outdoors with direct sun on the checkerboard, shade it before calibrating as excessive light reflection can make calibration harder.

**Checkerboard dimensions:**
- If you can print large format, print the [Tabloid](/img/docs/getting-started/tabloid-checkerboard.pdf) or [A3](/img/docs/getting-started/a3-checkerboard.pdf) board (50mm squares) and use it for every session, indoors and out. One board means the square size in your settings never has to change.
- The [Letter](/img/docs/getting-started/letter-checkerboard.pdf) or [A4](/img/docs/getting-started/a4-checkerboard.pdf) board (35mm squares) is only for indoor, small capture volumes — it is too small to calibrate reliably outdoors or when the cameras are far apart.
- Print at 100% scale and ensure the checkerboard has a few centimeters of white border around all sides.
- **Always measure your printed squares** and update the checkerboard dimensions in your account settings if they are not exactly 35mm or 50mm. Printers rarely reproduce the size exactly, and a 3% error in square size puts a 3% error on every distance you measure. See [Checkerboard](/docs/getting-started#checkerboard) for details.
- **The number of columns and rows to enter in the web app is the number of black-to-black corners**, not the number of squares.

**Checkerboard position:**
- The checkerboard must be visible to all cameras.
- It should stand perpendicular to the ground, with its long edge parallel to the ground.
- Keep it within 5m of the cameras.
- It should not point straight at one of the cameras — move it up, down, left, or right to avoid this.
- Position it at the center of your capture volume, at least 2m from the cameras, while visible by all cameras.


## Data Collection

- Tight clothing that contrasts with the background, and shoes that contrast with both the floor and clothing, improve tracking.
- Start recording at least 1 second before the participant begins the movement.
